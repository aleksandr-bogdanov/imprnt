imprnt is plain **markdown** files on your computer. No database, no cloud, no app to log into. You talk to your assistant, and it drives imprnt underneath.

A model costs money every time it thinks, and it forgets everything between sessions. So imprnt gives it one job, the one that needs real **understanding**, and keeps it out of the parsing, the sorting and the searching. Which side a step lands on comes down to how often it runs.

## When you write: the model works

Hand over a **transcript or a document**, and imprnt runs **one pass of four steps**: the model reads it, decides what it is, writes the summary, and picks the tags.

**Where a note lands** follows the rules in [Vault layout](/vault-layout/), and the model pays that cost **exactly once** per source.

## Edits are cheap, originals are forever

<HowItWorksEdits />

**One edit** fixes a fact everywhere it appears. A rename is looked up by alias, so the correction lands in the existing note and {'"Carl"'} stays in the aliases for old references. Every other note points at that person by their permanent file ID, so one fix carries the new name and role across every meeting and project.

A contradiction never overwrites. The next pass flags it for review and stamps the stale line **superseded**, so a reader can see which fact won.

**Your raw sources are kept forever**, one untouched snapshot per source under `raw/`. To change how notes are structured, re-run the model over the originals: you pay the model once more and get the new layout across the whole vault, so you are never stuck in an old format. Any claim traces back to its snapshot.

## When you read: plain code works

Ranking is arithmetic, so **no model touches it**. `recall` scores every note with BM25, <HowItWorksChip>a formula from the 1990s, pure arithmetic, no server.</HowItWorksChip> Three things set the score:

<HowItWorksRanking />

The model only turns your question into search words at the start and reads the few best notes at the end. Nothing AI-made sits under the search: no embeddings, no vectors (numeric codes a model would have to compute for every note). `recall` hands back the **top 15**.

Three things sit on top of BM25, **all on by default**, each fixing a blind spot with more arithmetic:

- **Stemming** folds English inflections, so a note about *swimming* answers a search for *swim*, and *policies* answers *policy*. Any word not ending in a Latin letter is left alone, so Russian, Greek and CJK are untouched.
- **Coverage** rewards matching more *distinct* words from your query, because BM25 adds up its score per word and a note repeating one word could outrank a note that matched three.
- **Proximity** rewards your words appearing near each other, because BM25 discards positions entirely: *double charge* side by side scores the same as two hundred words apart.

Together they lift retrieval (the right note in first position) from 19.7% to 24.9% on the LoCoMo benchmark, at no measurable cost: 157 ms before, 134 ms after, on a real 308-note vault. Turn any of them off with `--no-stem`, `--no-coverage` or `--no-proximity`.

## Why no search server

Every tool call the model makes costs **tokens**, the units AI work is billed in, whatever the wires look like. The two levers are payload size and caching.

<HowItWorksServer />

A live server answering search queries breaks both levers, so there is no query layer over the vault. A plugin's `sync` is fine: one batched call that caches locally.

## Core plus plugins

Always present: the core, meaning the vault plus `ingest`, `recall` and `check`. Everything else is an optional **plugin** you add or delete with one command, under one rule: the core never depends on a plugin. The how is in [How plugins work](/plugins/), the why in [Design decisions](/design-decisions/).
