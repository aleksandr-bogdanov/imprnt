Two kinds of "memory" get jumbled together: what you **know**, and rules for how the assistant should behave. imprnt keeps one store for knowledge and one strict rule about what loads on its own, so you can always say where a fact went.

## Two jobs people keep mixing up

A store does one of two jobs. The trouble starts when one tries to do both, or several do the same one.

<MemoryVaultSplit />

Force both into one bucket and it becomes a **junk drawer**.

A house style or an anti-slop ruleset is a **behavior plugin**: a fixed chunk of text you wire in with one import line in `CLAUDE.local.md`, your per-machine on-off file. It loads because you added the line, and it leaves when you delete it ([plugins](/plugins/)). The vault holds zero behavior.

## One store, three ways in

The **vault** is the only place knowledge lives: plain files on your disk, readable in any editor, and nothing about them pushes itself into a session. Everything reaches it one of three ways, each opened on demand.

<MemoryVaultDoors client:load />

`recall` is the everyday way in, ranking with BM25 ([how it works](/how-it-works/)). The assistant runs `imprnt context` right before it writes, so the **heavy filing rules** are paid for only by the sessions that write. A session inside the vault project, like `imp lair`, loads the contract natively from its `CLAUDE.md`.

An `imp` session loads almost nothing: your behavior plugins, plus a **~200-token pointer** saying the vault exists and how to reach it. A day of reading costs that pointer, never the whole vault.

<MemoryVaultTable>

| Surface | What it holds | Auto-loaded | Reached by | Who writes it |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| `vault/` notes | your knowledge | no | `recall` | you, through the model at filing |
| `hot.md` | the primer plus needs-review | no | `imprnt hot` | the model, as you work |
| the vault contract | the filing rules | in the vault project only | `imprnt context` | imprnt ships it |
| the session pointer | "the vault exists, search it" | yes | injected by `imp` | imprnt |
| `raw/` | untouched source copies | no | never searched | code, at filing |

</MemoryVaultTable>

## Your assistant has its own memory, so keep it thin

imprnt usually runs under Claude Code, which writes itself a `MEMORY.md` per project. The first stretch of that file auto-loads every session and lives outside `vault/`, so `recall` never sees it: a second **always-on** store you cannot search. One rule keeps it from becoming a parallel mess:

<MemoryThinRule />

The easy mistake is parking searchable knowledge in the store that cannot be searched. While building imprnt, project facts about the website and a plugin landed in the assistant's memory and had to be moved to be findable. Keep that memory down to a few behavior facts.

## Where each thing goes

<MemoryVaultTable>

| You want to keep | Put it in | Auto-loaded | Found by recall |
|------------------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| A fact about a person, policy, or decision | a vault note | no | yes |
| A number, table, or record | a vault note, in full | no | yes |
| A primer of where things stand | `hot.md`, via `imprnt hot` | no | no - control file |
| The filing rules | the vault contract, via `imprnt context` | in the vault project only | no |
| A writing style or anti-slop ruleset | a behavior plugin in `CLAUDE.local.md` | yes | no |
| A standing instruction | the assistant's `CLAUDE.md` or its memory | yes | no |
| Source transcripts and documents | a `raw/` copy | no | no |

</MemoryVaultTable>

## The mess imprnt set out to avoid

imprnt grew out of [PAI](https://github.com/danielmiessler/PAI), which pointed the way and also showed what happens when **three stores** all read like memory:

<MemoryVaultCollapse />

Each store loaded differently, and the jobs overlapped: USER and KNOWLEDGE both answered "who am I and what am I working on". The result was that you could rarely say where a fact lived or whether the assistant would see it.

One question routes anything you want to keep: knowledge goes to the vault, behavior goes to the config. The longer record of these calls is in [design decisions](/design-decisions/).
